A useful world description does not have to reveal what the characters have yet to discover. Trouble starts when one document serves as the GM notebook, player encyclopedia and draft for the next adventure. One careless edit can move a secret into shared material.

In Narrative Framework, a wiki belongs to a campaign. Its articles can supply several adventures while players receive a separately published version.

## Start with things your table returns to

You do not need to describe a continent first. Begin with three articles: the party's meeting place, an important character and an organization involved in their business. Add what is likely to appear at the table, then name the relationships.

For example, Mira the cartographer lives in the port and works for a guild. Articles let you read the setting as a wiki; the relationship map shows its connections. Both views use the same records, so there is no separate diagram to maintain.

## Separate descriptions from secrets

Use the ordinary body for information you intend to share. Put Mira's work forging maps for smugglers in GM notes or a separate private article. GM notes do not enter the player publication.

Treat relationships the same way. Knowing that the cartographer and the guild exist does not mean the characters know about their arrangement. Relationships have independent sharing settings. A player sees an edge only when both articles and the relationship itself are available to them.

## A saved draft is not a publication

After saving an article, open **Player publication**. Choose all campaign players or specific members, then deliberately publish it. Editing the draft later does not immediately change what the group reads.

Before a session, you can expand the port's history, prepare a mystery and revise descriptions while players keep the previously approved version. New information appears only after republishing. Review the title and written links as well as the body: the application cannot identify a secret placed in an ordinary paragraph.

## Preview a particular player's view

Send single-use invitations with the player role. Invite co-GMs separately: their role grants access to every draft and GM secret.

Use **Preview as player** before sharing a link. Check the index, article and map. If only one character discovered Mira's debt, publish that article to the relevant player. The rest of the party can still read her public description without learning the debt.

## Bring the world into an adventure

You can copy a saved article into your own adventure. The copy records its source version but does not silently follow later wiki edits. A prepared session therefore stays stable while you continue working on the setting.

Withdrawing publication or removing a member prevents further wiki access. It cannot retract an earlier download or a separately published play assignment. Agree with the group about the difference between player knowledge and character knowledge.
